# Astro-Resistence

Astrophotografy is gaining momentum each day. No doubt. But, I do not share the mainstream direction this community of [wanderers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pale_Blue_Dot_\(book\)) who seem to be heading to some commercial, I do not know, kind of "social network" competitive, zero-effort race. Expensive rigs, expensive software, all-detail full-coloured imaging. Everything tinted in this anodized red color of mainstream, almost dominator, seller. I get the point of zero-effort, because understanding how a whole rig and post-processing tools work takes much time. But I think that we are missing the main goal. Since the early observations of the movements of celestial bodies, say in Egypt and Mesopotamia 2000 B.C., the goal of sky watchers has always been "to understand". And, as an immediate consequence, to a deeper understanding of life. Many unanswered questions on Earth might have the answer up there, in the night skies. As Carl Sagan said, as a humbling experience. I do not see the point in transforming this path into a money burning madness. It is true, the most money you expend on your rig, the better the results you get. Unquestionable. But are you sure that you get the maximum out your current equipment. Besides, If you want to move to the next level, something completely understandable too, are there other choices, more affordable, to those high-end, red shining products out there?. Do I need to pay a 4 figures price for the next piece of equipment? I do ot have that money, and I do not think an autoguiding camera is worth paying 200 bucks (mine is under 40, DIY). Do _everybody_ need an harmonic mount?  In my case, my main tube is a 560mm focal length, 80mm aperture, doublet, and, since I do not "need" to get any further than this (the night sky is plenty of beauty, wonderful targets and challenges under its reach). My mount has been, for the majority of images shared here an SkyWatcher Star Adventurer, not a mount, but a tracker. Enough for me. Last year moved into a (second hand) Skywatcher EQ3 with OnStep motors and I am trying to re-image some of the targets. I know, for certain, that if I decided to move to a more powerful focal length, that rig would likey not be enough. But I am not done yet exploring the skies with my current 560mm and, sometimes 180mm Canon lens.

I am no one to tell you how to spend your money, everybody is free to make that decision. My main point is that, if you need to advance your skills and your results, do you really need a huge investment? I try to answer these questions in this place. The section about stories explains my understanding of what I see in my images. Nothing new, they are rather my field notes than something to share, but here they are if one wants to have a look. The section about hacks describes how I have moved on, by anylizing my needs and spending only the necessary with some hacks and tricks that everyone may follow as well.
